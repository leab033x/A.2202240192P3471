The GREAT Scottish Rent Trap: Is There a Future for Young Scots?

For young people under the age of 30 in Scotland, the promise of independence and financial security after university is increasingly overshadowed by a constant state of uncertainty. As property prices and rental costs continue to outpace wage growth, an entire generation is finding themselves at a breaking point.

This graph illustrates the proportion of a typical graduate income consumed by housing costs.

The financial reality for those entering the workforce in Scotland has become increasingly brutal. Once rent is deducted from a standard graduate starting salary, very little remains for essential costs, let alone a life outside of work. Scottish graduates are being forced into a monthly cycle of difficult sacrifices.

A comparison of rent-to-income ratios for graduates across Scotland’s four major cities.

The result of this crisis isn't just a tighter budget, it's forcing an entire generation out the door. As the rent to income ratios become unsustainable, the pressure becomes too much and we are seeing an entire generation being forced out our country.

The scale of the youth outflow: In 2025, 76% of all British citizens emigrating were under the age of 35.

This reveals a sobering reality: Scotland is reaching a point where its young talent may no longer be able to afford to live in its most vibrant economic centers. Without urgent attention to housing policy, the "anxiety" felt by those under 30 will continue to drive migration.
