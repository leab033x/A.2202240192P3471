The GREAT Scottish Rent Trap: Is There a Future for Young Scots?

For young people under the age of 30 in Scotland, the promise of independence and financial security after university is increasingly becoming corrupt by a constant state of uncertainty. As property prices and rental costs continue to outpace wage growth, an entire generation is finding themselves at a breaking point. According to the Scottish Government’s research, housing affordability and the rising cost of living have emerged as the primary concerns for young people looking toward the next two decades. This trend isn't just a temporary struggle in the market, the crisis runs deeper. It is a structural issue in our society that is breeding hardship and inequality making them a permanent part of life in Scotland. For many students finishing their degrees this year, the choice is starting to look pretty bleak: stay here and struggle to keep our heads above water, or leave the country to find a place where they can actually afford to live. The heart of the problem is that the numbers just don't align. The pay for graduate jobs does not meet the current needs of young graduates. The cost of renting a simple flat has gone through the roof. They are expected to start their professional lives with salaries that haven't kept up with the new reality of what it actually costs to rent in Scotland's major cities

The financial reality for those entering the workforce in Scotland has become increasingly brutal. Once rent is deducted from a standard graduate starting salary, very little remains for essential costs, let alone a life outside of work

Scottish graduates are being forced into a monthly cycle of difficult sacrifices choosing between secure housing and the ability to afford basic necessities. This constant pressure makes saving for a future deposit feel like a fantasy. It is completely unrealistic in this economy, as many young graduates find themselves simply struggling to survive the month in these major cities that are supposed to be hubs for beginning careers. The experiences that are traditionally supposed to define one’s twenties are being traded away just to keep a roof over their heads, heat in the home and food in their mouths. The crisis is not felt equally across the country. Scotland’s four major cities Glasgow, Edinburgh, Dundee, and Aberdeen each present unique challenges for the modern graduate. While Edinburgh continues to be the most expensive market, cities like Glasgow are experiencing rapid rent increases that are quickly erasing their reputation for being affordable. By looking at rent-to-income ratios across these major hubs, it becomes clear exactly where it is most financially challenging to start a professional career in Scotland.
