The GREAT Scottish Rent Trap: Is There a Future for Young Scots?

For young people under the age of 30 in Scotland, the promise of independence and financial security after university is increasingly overshadowed by a constant state of uncertainty. As property prices and rental costs continue to outpace wage growth, an entire generation is finding themselves at a breaking point. According to the Scottish Government’s research, housing affordability and the rising cost of living have emerged as the primary concerns for young people looking toward the next two decades.

This trend isn't just a temporary struggle in the market, the crisis runs deeper. It is a structural issue in our society that is breeding hardship and inequality making them a permanent part of life in Scotland. For many students finishing their degrees this year, the choice is starting to look pretty bleak: stay here and struggle to keep our heads above water, or leave the country to find a place where they can actually afford to live.

The heart of the problem is that the numbers just don't align. The pay for graduate jobs does not meet the current needs of young graduates. The cost of renting a simple flat has gone through the roof. They are expected to start their professional lives with salaries that haven't kept up with the new reality of what it actually costs to rent in Scotland's major cities.

This graph illustrates the proportion of a typical graduate income consumed by housing costs, highlighting the limited "lifestyle" budget remaining after essential bills.

The financial reality for those entering the workforce in Scotland has become increasingly brutal. Once rent is deducted from a standard graduate starting salary, very little remains for essential costs, let alone a life outside of work.

Scottish graduates are being forced into a monthly cycle of difficult sacrifices choosing between secure housing and the ability to afford basic necessities. This constant pressure makes saving for a future deposit feel like a fantasy. It is completely unrealistic in this economy, as many young graduates find themselves simply struggling to survive the month in these major cities that are supposed to be hubs for beginning careers.

A comparison of rent-to-income ratios for graduates across Scotland’s four major cities, identifying where housing stress is most drastic.

The result of this crisis isn't just a tighter budget, it's forcing an entire generation out the door. As the rent to income ratios in cities like Edinburgh and Glasgow become unsustainable, the pressure becomes too much and we are seeing an entire generation being forced out our country.

This data shows that the demographic most affected by the rent trap is those under 35. This demographic makes up the overwhelming majority of British citizens choosing to build their lives elsewhere. We aren't just losing workers; we are losing the future of our communities.

The scale of the youth outflow: In 2025, 76% of all British citizens emigrating were under the age of 35. Each icon represents 5,000 individuals deciding that the cost of staying is simply too high.

This reveals a sobering reality: Scotland is reaching a point where its young talent may no longer be able to afford to live in its most vibrant economic centers. Without urgent attention to housing policy and a realignment of wages with the actual cost of living, the "anxiety" felt by those under 30 will continue to drive migration and deepen social inequality.
